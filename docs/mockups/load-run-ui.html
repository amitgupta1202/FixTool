The Load Run, Redrawn

The load run works. It does not look like it works. This is the second draw of its two screens, the dialog and the document, with every change named and placed.

Version 1.17.0 shipped the load run: two doors into one dialog, a live document, a record on disk, a CLI and two MCP tools. The engine is sound. The screens are not, and the reason is narrower than "ugly".

One root cause. There is no type scale and no visual rank. In LoadRunDialog.kt and LoadRunDocument.kt almost every string is drawn at 10sp or 11sp in textSecondary or textDisabled grey. The dialog is eight rows of identical weight. The document is five tiles of identical weight followed by four dot-separated sentences. Nothing tells the eye where to land, so both read as a report that happens to be inside a window rather than a screen you operate.

One aggravating factor. The controls are drawn by hand. Choice() at LoadRunDialog.kt:248 is a Text prefixed with "◉ " or "○ " plus a bare selectable modifier. To be precise about what that does and does not cost, because the first draft of this note got it wrong: the app is wrapped in MaterialTheme (WindowChrome.kt:34), so selectable is focusable, toggles on Space and Enter, and draws the ripple's hover, focus and press layers. What is actually missing is Role.RadioButton semantics, so a screen reader is never told it is a radio, any affordance beyond one character, and a hit target larger than the glyph plus its label. The same two glyphs also serve the multi-select "Also match on" row, so radio semantics are used for a checkbox job.

Worth saying plainly, because it shapes the fix: the shipped screens are a step down from their own mockup. Mockup C had real radio inputs and chips for the listen set. Mockup D had a bucketed round-trip histogram and a wire › affordance on every unanswered row. None of that survived the build. So most of what follows is not new design, it is design that was agreed and then lost.

The document

The example is one run against the RFQ venue: 500/s for 10m on five lanes, 300,000 QuoteRequests, every one answered. It passes. It also has two four-second stalls in it, at t+42s and t+318s, where replies fell to about 320 a second and p95 round trip went from 4ms to 210ms.

Which chart you get follows the issue span, not the shape. A 4,000 burst leaves in 813ms, so perSecond holds three buckets and the strip draws three bars that say nothing. It is not hidden: the gate is if (report.perSecond.size > 1) at LoadRunDocument.kt:90, and three is more than one. But shape is the wrong discriminator anyway, because a ×300,000 burst on five lanes issues for about a minute and has a real per-second story, while a 500/s rate run has a distribution worth a curve. So: the per-second panel appears when issue.spanMs is more than a few seconds, and the round-trip curve appears always, in the stat card's slot.

correction An earlier draft of this note said both the curve and the bucket histogram "need no new collection" because roundTripsSorted is sorted in finish(). That is true of the matcher and false of the report. LoadRunner.kt:256 reduces the samples to roundTrip = sorted?.let { RunSetStats.of(it) }, and RunSetStats.Distribution is seven numbers (RunSetStats.kt:35). No sample reaches LoadReport or load.json, so neither picture can be drawn from what is recorded. The line below it is worse for the live view: perSecond is emptyList() for the whole run, because result is only non-null in the final report, and pendingPeak is 0 until then. The "Proposed · running" panel has no data source at all today. Both are engine work, and both are now in the order of work.

What the engine work is, and why it is small. Not persisting 300,000 samples. A fixed log-spaced round-trip histogram of about 30 buckets from 0.1ms to 100s, held in StampMatcher and incremented once per matched reply inside the critical section that already exists at :167, then carried on LoadReport and written by the codec. Thirty integers bound the memory whatever the run's size, they give the curve its 30 points and the stat card its buckets, and because they are incremental they are correct during the run as well as after it. The exact percentiles keep coming from the sorted array at finish(), so nothing about the numbers already reported changes. The same move fixes the live chart: maintain the per-second counters and each second's p95 as stamps arrive rather than rebuilding them in buckets(), and expose a bucketsSoFar() beside the existing roundTripsSoFar().

That is the whole argument for the redraw. Today's document shows one stall badly and the other not at all. The first is four bars of weight(1f) dipping to about 46% of a 36dp strip, with no reference line, no axis and no latency beside them, so you can see that something moved but not what moved or by how much. The second is past MAX_STRIP_SECONDS = 120 and is never drawn. Meanwhile LoadReport.Second.p95Us is measured every second and written into load.json, and nothing in the app has ever drawn it.

Keyboard, and what the focus ring lands on

The redraw's case rests partly on real controls, so it owes an account of what "real" buys. Tab order follows the reading order: template picker, view message, profile picker, shape segment, count, the four preset chips, settle, the Advanced disclosure, then the footer. Inside Advanced, the two tag fields, the reply-type picker, the listen checkboxes, the store radios. Space toggles a segment, a chip, a checkbox and a radio. Arrow keys move within the shape segment and within the radio group, which is what makes a radio group a group rather than two independent buttons, and is precisely what Modifier.selectable does not give today.

Esc cancels and Ctrl+Enter (⌘Enter on macOS) runs. The footer hint has to read ⌘↵ or Ctrl+↵ per platform: this app ships on Mac and Windows, and the mockups above show only the Mac form. The focus ring is the app's existing AppTheme.Colors.primary at 1dp, the same one SlimField already draws on focus, so focus looks like one thing across the dialog rather than a Material ripple on some rows and a border on others.

How Compare works

Everything above is a redraw of what exists. Compare is the only addition, and it is small, because both runs are already on disk: it reads two load.json records and subtracts them. Nothing is rerun, so a run the CLI made overnight compares against one you fired in the app an hour ago.

Where it opens from
"Compare…" in the document header, which lists the runs comparable with this one, or two runs selected in Recent. Both read from ~/.fixtool/loads/<id>/load.json.
What makes two runs comparable
The two plans must agree on the exchange being measured: same request MsgType, same match tags, same reply type. Those three decide what a round trip is. Everything else may differ and is shown as context rather than as a delta: lane count, shape, rate, settle window, store choice.
What it refuses, and why that is the point
If the match differs, Compare names the difference instead of showing a delta. A run matching on 131 QuoteReqID and one matching on 11 ClOrdID measured two different things, and a tool that will cheerfully subtract them is a tool that produces confident wrong numbers.
What is compared
The counts (answered, unanswered, duplicates, late, strays), the six round-trip percentiles, the rate verdict and max lag, the whole tool block, and two derived measures the single-run document does not show: peak outstanding, and the number of seconds whose p95 crossed a threshold.
Which direction counts as better
Completeness up, latency down, tool counts down. Anything the tool cannot rank shows the pair and "same" or "changed", never an arrow. A percentage appears only where the earlier value is non-zero, so 77 → 0 reads "cleared" rather than "−100%".
The threshold row
"stalls over 100ms p95" counts seconds in perSecond whose p95Us exceeded the threshold. This one genuinely is free: the final report does carry perSecond with its per-second p95Us, unlike the round-trip samples. The threshold is editable and defaults to a fixed 100ms, applied identically to both runs. Deriving it per run (say 10× that run's own p50) would ask each run a different question and make the row meaningless.
Sets compare phase by phase
Aligned by position and name, one row per phase plus a set roll-up. A phase present in one set and not the other reads as added or removed, not as a delta from zero.

Load sets, and whether this design survives them

#45 asks for ordered phases under one seed with one report: burst QuoteRequests, then hit the quotes, then cancel what is left. The question is whether the redraw above survives that, or has to be undone for it. Nothing in this section is proposed as part of the redraw. #45 is its own issue and its own build. What the two figures below buy is the knowledge that the redraw is not a dead end, plus the four things #45 will need that are cheaper to know now than to discover later, one of which (LoadPlan.problems()) is worth doing while the dialog is already open.

It survives, and the reason is that the pieces were already cut that way. A phase in #45 carries its own template, issuing profile, listen profiles, match tags, reply type, shape and settle window, which is exactly the dialog's body. So the three groups are already a phase editor, and a set is the seed band, an ordered list of phases, and a policy. Per-session numbers

The report is aggregate today. Per-lane numbers are cheaper to collect than they look, because the lane is already in hand: StampMatcher.Pending carries laneSlot (StampMatcher.kt:42), record() already receives that Pending (:165), and every match already serialises on synchronized(samples) (:167). So a per-lane write is one int into a parallel list inside a critical section that already exists. At 300,000 samples that is 1.2MB beside the 2.4MB of round trips already kept, partitioned and sorted in finish() rather than during the run.

The cost is not performance, it is the screen, and the two halves of "per-session" are not equally worth showing.

  • Per-lane completeness is sound and useful. Unanswered, duplicates and matched per lane are unaffected by how the run was driven. Four unanswered requests all on one lane is a finding, and the data is already there: UnmatchedRequest.lane exists and the table above already prints the column.
  • Per-lane latency would partly measure FixTool. LoadRunner.kt:97 drives every lane from one pacer loop, rendering and sending on that thread, round-robin. With 50 lanes the last lane in a cycle is issued systematically later than the first by roughly 49 × the per-message cost. That is small against a 3ms p50 and it is systematic per lane, which is the exact shape of a false finding: lane 50 looks slightly worse forever, and somebody goes looking for it in the venue.
  • A 50 × 6 matrix answers no question anyone asks. The real question is "is any lane much worse than the rest", which is one sentence.

The type scale, which is the actual fix

The app has no named type scale. Sizes are literals at every call site, and the load screens use two of them for everything. Four roles cover both screens, and would give the rest of the app somewhere to converge.

tokensizeweightcarries
Type.figure26sp mono400the two counts that decide the verdict
Type.head13sp sans600the verdict badge, a section head
Type.body11.5sp sans400a row's value, a table cell, a refusal
Type.meta10sp mono400a group head, an axis label, a records path
And one colour change with it. Type.meta is drawn today in textDisabled #6A6A6A, which on #1E1E1E is about 3.1:1 and fails AA for 10sp text. Lighten textDisabled to about #8A8A8A (4.6:1) before a token makes that pairing the house rule. It affects the whole app, which is the point of doing it with the scale rather than after it.

Today's screens use Type.body and Type.meta only, with one 17sp exception inside Tile. That single missing rank is most of what reads as ugly.

What is not changing

  • Every refusal sentence, verbatim, including the em-dash in the far-end notice. They name the cause and the fix, they are the best copy in the app, and storeProblem() is deliberately owned in one place and shared by eight callers so surfaces cannot drift (FixConnectionConfig.kt:92). Both panels above now quote what ships. An earlier draft of this note paraphrased them, which is exactly the drift that KDoc exists to prevent.
  • The three separate judgements, completeness, rate and tool. That model is right and only its presentation moves.
  • "Issued is three numbers" (requested, handed to the engine, left the socket). It moves to the quiet strip, and stays.
  • The far-end notice, which warns and does not refuse.
  • Every testTag, so the tests keep pointing at the same things. Their assertions do change, and pretending otherwise was wrong: LoadRunDocumentTest asserts load-state contains "unmatched 4", load-verdict contains "UNMATCHED 4 of 4,000" and "RUNNING", and load-unmatched-label contains "pending", all of which this note renames to "unanswered", "ISSUING" and "outstanding". LoadRunDialogTest clicks load-store-profile, which now lives inside a collapsed Advanced. Updating them is part of the work, and the store test is the reason Advanced has to open itself when something inside it is addressed.

Order of work

  1. Controls and scale. The slim radio, checkbox and segmented control in SlimComponents.kt, the four Type tokens in Theme.kt, and textDisabled lightened to clear AA. Nothing load-specific, and the other dialogs that use the glyph trick can follow later.
  2. The dialog, and LoadPlan.problems() with it. Groups, Advanced that opens itself, pinned footer, inline refusals, presets, persistence. The refusals move out of the composable in the same pass, because they are what Advanced keys off and because #45 needs them headless.
  3. The document. Verdict header, leads and strip, 8dp progress, the stat card, actionable unanswered rows, and the four edge states.
  4. The measurements the charts need. A 30-bucket log histogram on LoadReport and in the codec, incremented in StampMatcher; per-second counters and per-second p95 maintained incrementally with a bucketsSoFar(); per-lane completeness counts. Engine work, no UI, and the reason the charts moved from step 4 to step 5.
  5. The charts. Compose Canvas: the per-second panel with its p95 panel and stall guides, and the outstanding curve on log-log, gated on issue.spanMs.
  6. One record shape. A run becomes a one-phase set on disk, with a reader for the records already in ~/.fixtool/loads. Before Compare, because Compare reads that schema.
  7. Compare, its own tab, with the not-comparable state built first.
  8. The render-ahead producer, then per-lane latency. Each lane renders ahead of its own sends, so the per-lane numbers stop being a picture of the pacer's round-robin. Only then is per-lane latency collected and shown.

Steps 1 to 3 answer "it is ugly" and depend on nothing else. Steps 4 to 7 answer "it is not intuitive", and step 4 is the one an earlier draft of this note wrongly called free. Step 8 is there because a number worth showing has to be made honest first, which is a step and not a caveat.

Decisions

Every question below was put with options and a recommendation on 2026-09-07. The nine marked decided were answered, and each took the recommendation. The four marked assumed were not put, because the recommendation carries little risk and is reversible: they stand unless overruled. The one marked superseded was decided and then withdrawn, because its answer was a deferral: it put a number on screen that this note itself calls a false finding, and hid it well enough that the cause would never get fixed.

The redraw

decidedBespoke slim controls, or Material3 scaled down?
Bespoke, in SlimComponents.kt. Material3's RadioButton is 20dp with a 48dp minimum interactive size and will not sit in a 24dp row. The app already made this call for text fields and buttons.
assumedShould Advanced be collapsed by default?
Collapsed when everything inside it is fine, open whenever a refusal names anything inside it. Match is inferred whenever the template carries a correlation tag and the memory store is the recommendation, so the common case is a one-line summary. The rule is not limited to a failed inference: the store problem, the reply type and the listen set all live in there too, and a collapsed group hiding the control a refusal points at would break the claim that refusals sit next to their cause. It also has to yield to a test, since LoadRunDialogTest clicks load-store-profile.
decidedThe chart: Compose Canvas, or a charting dependency?
Canvas. Two stacked panels, bars, a step line and a polyline. It is under 200 lines and adds no dependency to a tool that ships as a desktop bundle.
decidedCompare in the same document, or its own tab?
Its own tab, opened from the header. A document that sometimes has two columns is two layouts pretending to be one.
assumedIs "Copy as fixtool load" worth the button?
Yes. The gap between a run someone tuned by hand in the app and the line that gates their build is exactly this button, and the CLI already accepts every field the dialog collects.

Load sets (#45)

decidedIs a single run a set of one?
One shape on disk, two doors in the UI. Make the record a set with one phase so there is one reader, one JSON schema and one Compare. Do not make anyone build a set to fire one burst: "Load run…" stays exactly as it is, and saves as a one-phase set behind the scenes. Sequencing consequence: this changes the load.json schema that both the document and Compare read, so it has to land before Compare rather than with #45, and it needs a reader for the records already in ~/.fixtool/loads. It is therefore in the order of work below, not in #45's.
decidedShould the seed be the run id only, or can a phase read values an earlier phase received? (#45 raises this itself)
Run id only, and make the other one its own issue. Reading a venue-minted OrderID out of phase one needs a capture rule per phase, somewhere to put the captured values, and a policy for what happens when a capture is missing. That is a scenario feature wearing a load feature's clothes, and the three flows in #45 do not need it: derivable ids (${run} plus ${messageIndex}) cover all of them.
decidedShould phase 2 address only the ids phase 1 actually matched?
No by default, and offer it later as an opt-in. Addressing all 4,000 keeps the ids derivable, which is the whole reason no state has to pass between phases. It is also often the better test: a cancel for an order the venue never acknowledged is a case worth firing on purpose. When somebody wants the other behaviour, "only ids the previous phase matched" is a checkbox on the phase, not a redesign.
decidedWhat is the default when a phase fails?
Stop and skip the rest, as #45 proposes, and report the skipped phases rather than dropping them. A set that quietly reports two phases when three were asked for is worse than one that says "skipped".
decidedOne continuous throughput chart across the phases, or one per phase?
Per phase, plus a set-level timeline. I argued for the continuous chart first and it is wrong: phases are mostly settle, so a shared time axis spends 95% of its width on empty seconds and squeezes the interesting 0.8s into eight pixels. The timeline says where the clock went in three rows, and each phase keeps its own chart on its own axis.

Compare

assumedMore than two runs at once?
Two. Two runs is a question ("did the fix work"). Three is a table, and at that point the answer is Copy JSON and a spreadsheet.
assumedShould Compare offer to rerun the older plan?
Yes, as one button, and only when the plan is still valid. "Run this plan again" is the natural next click after seeing a regression, and the plan is in the record. It should refuse when the template or profile the plan names no longer exists, rather than silently substituting.

Per-session numbers

decidedCollect per-lane, and show how much?
Collect all of it, put all of it in load.json, show it. Collection is one int per matched reply inside a critical section that already exists, so there is no reason to decide against it. On screen: per-lane completeness beside the unanswered table, where it is already half present, and per-lane latency in a sortable lane table.
supersededFix the pacer first, or ship the lane latency with a caveat?
Fix the pacer. The earlier answer was to ship it behind a drill-down and not headline it until the render-ahead producer existed. That is a deferral: it puts a number on screen that this very note calls a false finding, and hides it well enough that nobody fixes the cause. LoadRunner.kt:97 renders and sends every lane from one pacer loop, so lane N is issued systematically later than lane 1 by roughly (N−1) × the per-message cost. Give each lane a render-ahead producer so its sends are not queued behind every other lane's rendering, and the per-lane numbers become worth showing plainly. Until that exists, per-lane latency is not collected and not shown. Per-lane completeness is unaffected by the ordering and ships either way.
