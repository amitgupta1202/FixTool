Two parties, one venue

A buy side asks, a dealer prices, the buy side lifts, and the venue in the middle carries every message across, re-keying the ids so each side only sees its own. Today FixTool can sit on both sides of a real venue, but it can't be that venue.

“Buy side sends a buy request, then sell side prices (Quote), then buy side accepts. 2 clients involved. How does our FI RFQ venue work?” The question, 13 Sep 2026

What changes

The bundled Fixed Income RFQ Desk answers every request itself. Its two clients are both requesters, and nothing links one client's session to another's: a reply always goes back to whoever sent the trigger, and every book is kept per counterparty. The proposal adds the one path that's missing.

Nothing on the dealer's side shares a value with the buy side's. That's why a relay has to be an edge the venue records decision 9: the union-find that Trace runs on shared ids would see two unrelated conversations. The one id that crosses on purpose is 37=V-T-1042, on both confirmations, so the two sides can be reconciled decision 8. Lanes are ordered requesters, venue, responders when the venue declares roles. Venue ids are shortened for reading; the rules draw them with ${req.uuid} and ${uuid:10}.

Who plays what part

The venue learns roles from a list, not from behaviour. A dealer that sends a QuoteRequest is misconfigured, and the platform should say so (658=6, not authorized to request quote) instead of relaying it. The RFQ book lives on the venue's own pane because it spans counterparties.

Rules that address someone else

Three things are new on a rule card. A step has a To, which defaults to the sender, so every rule written before this still means what it meant. The trigger has three new rows: the sender's role, the RFQ's state, and whether any responder is online. And the card shows who a step reaches, so nobody has to connect four sessions to find out.

Test before anyone connects

The dry run already shows which rule wins and what it would send. With relays, it also needs to know who sent the message, because the role decides the rule. And it has to show every recipient, including an address that resolves to nobody.

An address that reaches nobody isn't an error. A single-dealer RFQ has no cover. It's reported the same way a responder who isn't logged on is: nothing sent, and said so decision 2, 7.

Both sides in one scenario

Scenarios need no change: every step already names its session. What changes is that there's now a venue to run them against, so the example can ship scenarios that assert what each party saw. Captured values carry the re-keyed ids from step to step.

Step 8 is the one that proves the venue. It asserts the dealer's fill carries the same 37 the buy side got, on the opposite side. Before this, no example could fail that assertion, because no example had two parties to a trade.

The example it becomes

Recommended: the Fixed Income RFQ Desk becomes the platform. A single-dealer RFQ is already what the FX RFQ Venue shows, and that venue picks up the three corrections it still owes from the FI desk. Examples are copied once, so the fixed-income-rfq folder you have keeps the desk until you reset it.

Open questions

These are market questions more than engineering ones, and each one changes the example rather than the engine.

  1. Disclosed or anonymous? Does the buy side see which dealer quoted, and the dealer who asked?Proposed: disclosed both ways, via Parties (452=35 to the buy side, 452=13 to the dealer).
  2. Firm or last look? On a lift, does the platform trade at once or ask the dealer to confirm?Proposed: firm. A subject variant is two more rules, not a feature.
  3. Who chooses the dealers?Proposed: every responder online. Dealers named in the request's Parties would be a small follow-up address.
  4. Counters. Is a 694=2 relayed back for another round?Proposed: not in slice B; one round first.
  5. Cover. Is the runner-up told Cover and everyone else Done Away?Proposed: yes, as drawn above.
  6. Replace the FI desk, or add a sixth example?Proposed: replace.
  7. On a firm platform, how is the dealer told its quote traded? A FIX 4.4 ExecutionReport has no QuoteID.Answered: on BrokerTec the dealer gets both, and a TradeCaptureReport goes out for the trade too, with settings that can switch some off. The example sends all three to each side.
